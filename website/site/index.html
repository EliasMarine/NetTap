The problem

Your network never stops talking. Your router barely listens.

More devices than you can name

TVs, cameras, speakers and laptops reach the internet all day. Few of them tell you who they are talking to.

Routers show totals at best

A home router gives you rough traffic numbers. Products that go deeper often keep the detail behind a subscription.

Pro tools expect a pro

Open-source security stacks are powerful, but setting them up and reading their output takes real Linux and security experience.

See what NetTap does

The solution

One small box that watches, records and explains.

Put NetTap on your network and it builds a full record of what every device does, then turns it into a dashboard anyone in the house can read.

  • Every connection, DNS lookup and TLS certificate
  • Intrusion detection with plain-English explanations
  • Full packet capture you can search and download

Organized around the question you actually ask: what are my devices doing?

Devices

Every device on the network with its traffic, alerts and a risk score. Name them the way your household does.

Alerts

Suricata detections and behavior checks such as beaconing and DNS anomalies, each explained in a sentence or two.

Packets

Full PCAP you can search and download, with TShark and CyberChef built in for when you need the raw detail.

How NetTap reads your traffic

  • Checks it against threat rules.Suricata matches traffic against Emerging Threats rules.
  • Explains what it found.Each alert says what happened, which device and what to do next.

Packet analysis

Ask a question. Get the packets that answer it.

NetTap keeps the packets, not just the records. Pick a question or write a filter, and TShark reads the capture and shows you, packet by packet, what crossed your network.

Start with a common question

  • Which sites did a device open?
  • What did a device look up?
  • Was anything sent without encryption?
  • Why did a connection fail?
  • Did a name fail to look up?

How it connects

Three ways onto your network. Pick one in the setup wizard.

Mirror / SPAN

Recommended
  • A managed switch copies traffic to NetTap.
  • NetTap sits outside the traffic path, so it can't break your network.

Also needs a management interface for the dashboard.

Inline bridge

Advanced · 2 NICs
  • NetTap sits between the modem and the router and sees all internet traffic.
  • The kernel bridge keeps forwarding if NetTap's services stop.

Sees traffic after the router's NAT, so every device shows as the router's IP.

DHCP gateway

In development
  • NetTap hands out addresses and routes traffic to your router, so it sees each device's own IP.
  • You turn off the router's DHCP. Devices lose internet if NetTap goes down.

Still being built and not yet tested on real hardware.

Low upkeep

NetTap checks the disk every 5 minutes and clears space before it fills.

80% 90% 95%
  • 80%Deletes data past its retention.
  • 90%Deletes the oldest captures, then the oldest records.
  • 95%Also pauses packet capture until usage drops below 85%.
  • Watches SSD health
  • Updates Malcolm with backup and rollback

Install

Four commands to a working dashboard.

  1. Run the installer

    It blinks each port's LED so you can tell them apart, installs Docker and starts the services.

  2. Open the dashboard

    Go to https://nettap.local from any device on your network.

  3. Finish the wizard

    Enter the one-time setup code, then pick the capture mode, ports, retention and an admin account.

Terminal · on the NetTap box 
$ git clone https://github.com/EliasMarine/NetTap.git
$ cd NetTap
$ git checkout v0.4.0-alpha.2
$ sudo scripts/install/install.sh --skip-bridge

Then open https://nettap.local

Full setup guide

Pre-release. The current version is v0.4.0-alpha.2, an alpha for early testers. The command above is for mirror / SPAN mode, the one tested on real hardware. Expect rough edges.
