# Plan Review Log: #8 quiet hours and a daily summary (NET-330)
Started 2026-11-20. MAX_ROUNDS=5. Plan: plans/2026-11-20-f8-quiet-hours.md

## Round 1 - Codex

The release logic needs revision. With the proposed algorithm, a healthy channel can lose its entire morning summary, send twice for one window, or deliver ordinary notifications during the following night.

### Dispatcher and scheduling

1. **High — Tomorrow’s alerts can evict today’s entire summary.** §4.3 keeps one severity-sorted `MAX_PENDING` cut. At 07:00 in daily mode, 50 fresh severity-2 alerts belonging to tomorrow can displace all 50 severity-3 alerts due today. The plan then records today’s alerts in `done`, leaving nothing to trigger their release. A channel-wide `dropped` counter also lets an intervening critical send consume the wrong window’s count. **Fix:** classify before trimming, bound the due and held groups independently, and attach overflow to the group that incurred it. Test full queues on both sides of 07:00. 2. **High — A successful send can permanently discard a cap warning.** `cap_queue` returns both the events to retain and the subset eligible to send. A warning for a temporarily changed or unreadable cap exists only in the retained list. §4.3 partitions `offered`, then reconstructs the queue from those partitions. An unrelated successful alert send therefore erases that warning. **Fix:** derive the next queue from the full retained list, removing only due entries acknowledged by this send. Test changing cap A to B, sending a critical alert successfully, then restoring cap A. 3. **High — An expired stamp bypasses every subsequent overnight window.** §4.3 checks `held_until <= now` first. A box stopped at 06:55 and restarted at 23:00 immediately sends its ordinary backlog during the next night’s hold. The same happens if a morning release fails and the endpoint recovers that night, since journal events retry indefinitely. **Fix:** associate the release with its window. On entry to a later overnight window, rehold noncritical items. Preserve same-release backoff in daily mode. Test both restart and transport recovery during the following night. ### Claude response

The plan is rewritten; its §10 lists each finding and what changed. In short:

- **Hold rule redesigned.** Overnight holds by the clock alone; daily mode stamps each item with when it happened (`held_since`) and releases it once a summary time has passed (#3, #6, #8).
- **Queues.** Due and held items are split before trimming, with separate counts (#1). The next event queue comes from cap_queue's full kept list (#2). Cap lines go first in a release (#4).
- **A fresh critical bypasses a failure back-off** (#5, in part).
- **Smaller fixes:** UTC comparisons (#7), ISO stamps (#9), backup preflight, validated export and a zone note (#10, #11), `tz` in GET and PUT (#13).
- **G11 check** runs in isolation with a parsing sink (#14, #15).
- **Rejected:** #5's summary waiting for the hourly slot or a back-off (a design choice, see D3), and #12 (the destination change is the owner's own action, behind the admin session).

## Round 3 - Codex

The revised plan still allows a critical-only send to mark the morning summary complete, and its intake gate can let tomorrow’s alerts evict today’s entire batch. Both failures occur on a healthy channel.

1. **High — A critical escalation can consume the daily release.** A severity-2 alert held overnight keeps its `held_since` when it escalates to severity 1. At 07:00, while intake is catching up, the critical can go through alone. Because §4.3 defines *any* message containing a stamped item as a release, that send advances `released_through` to 07:00. When intake finishes, the ordinary morning batch waits until tomorrow. **Fix:** mark a send explicitly as the scheduled batch for a particular cutoff. Only success of that batch may advance the watermark or clear its counters. Test an escalation during catch-up, followed by a restart. [Merge and eligibility rules](</Volumes/ExtraStrg_RAID1/Mega Sync/Coding Projects/NetTap/.claude/worktrees/f8/plans/2026-11-20-f8-quiet-hours.md:107>) · [Release acknowledgement](</Volumes/ExtraStrg_RAID1/Mega Sync/Coding Projects/NetTap/.claude/worktrees/f8/plans/2026-11-20-f8-quiet-hours.md:128>)

2. **High — Waiting for intake defeats the new due-first alert limit.** At 07:00, suppose 50 severity-3 alerts belong to the morning release and 50 new severity-2 alerts belong to tomorrow. One full journal page makes `caught_up` false, even if none of its entries route to this channel. `_due` then classifies all 100 alerts as held. Severity trimming discards all 50 morning alerts and records them in `done`. **Fix:** determine which alerts belong to the current release before trimming. Apply the intake gate only when deciding whether to transmit the retained batch. Test the two full alert groups with one unrelated journal page. [Plan eligibility and capacity rules](</Volumes/ExtraStrg_RAID1/Mega Sync/Coding Projects/NetTap/.claude/worktrees/f8/plans/2026-11-20-f8-quiet-hours.md:109>)

3. **High — Held alert counts can disappear or be reported on the wrong day.** Fifty critical alerts can take every slot, evict ten ordinary held alerts, and send immediately. Only `held_dropped=10` remains. The poll loop does not target that channel for its count, and `digest()` requires a nonempty item list. There may never be a morning message.
